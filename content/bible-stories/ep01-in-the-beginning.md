**Scripture version for on-screen quotes:** King James Version (KJV), public domain

## Beat 3 · Welcome (0:28)

**[VISUAL]** Narrator on camera, seated on a wooden bench under a mango tree in a Ghanaian compound. Late-afternoon light. Three children sit on a mat nearby. Lower-third: *[Narrator name] · Jesus Worldwide*.

>
> Here we tell the stories of the Bible, from the very first page all the way to Jesus. Because the Bible is not a basket of separate stories. It is one great story, and every part of it points to one Person.
>

## Beat 4 · "In the beginning" (1:09)

**[VISUAL]** Scripture card over a deep, starless black: **"In the beginning God created the heaven and the earth." · Genesis 1:1 (KJV)**. Slow push-in.

**NARRATION**
> "In the beginning God created the heaven and the earth."
>
> Ten words. And already they tell us something wonderful. Before anything else existed, God was there. Nobody made God. Every star, every river, every person began because God decided it should be.

---

## Beat 5 · Darkness, then light: Day One (1:26)

**[VISUAL]** Illustration: dark, restless water under a black sky, with a faint glow moving over the surface (the Spirit of God, shown only as light and wind). Then a flash: the whole frame floods with warm white-gold light.

**NARRATION**
> At first, the Bible says, "the earth was without form, and void; and darkness was upon the face of the deep." Imagine it. No shape. No colour. No sound of birds. Only deep, dark water. But even there, God was not far away, because "the Spirit of God moved upon the face of the waters."
>
> Then God spoke. No struggle, no long ceremony. Just a word.
>
> "And God said, Let there be light: and there was light."
>
> Just like that! Darkness had no argument. And God saw the light, that it was good. He called the light Day and the darkness Night. That was the evening and the morning: the first day.

---

## Beat 6 · Sky, sea and land: Days Two and Three (2:13)

**[VISUAL]** Illustration sequence with slow pans: (1) the waters dividing, with a wide blue sky opening above; (2) seas rolling back and dry land rising, reddish-brown earth like the laterite soil of Ghana; (3) a time-lapse feel as grass, cassava leaves, cocoa trees, baobabs and a great mango tree spring up.

**NARRATION**
> On the second day, God made the sky, a great wide space between the waters above and the waters below.
>
> On the third day, God gathered the waters into seas, and dry ground appeared. Then God said, let the earth bring out grass and plants and fruit trees, and it did. Picture a cocoa farm after the rains. The tall baobab in the north. Plantain, yam, groundnut, and yes, the mango tree we are sitting under. Every one of them was God's idea.
>
> And again, God saw that it was good.

---

## Beat 7 · Sun, moon and stars: Day Four (2:52)

**[VISUAL]** Illustration: a huge golden sun rising over a savannah; cross-fade to a silver moon; then a slow pull-back into a night sky so full of stars it looks like spilled sugar. Scripture card: **"…he made the stars also." · Genesis 1:16 (KJV)**.

**NARRATION**
> On the fourth day, God filled the sky with lights. The Bible says, "God made two great lights; the greater light to rule the day, and the lesser light to rule the night: he made the stars also."
>
> Children, listen to that last part. "He made the stars also." There are billions upon billions of stars, and the Bible mentions them in just five words, as if God is saying, "Oh, and the stars. I made those too." That is how great our God is.

---

## Beat 8 · Fish, birds and animals: Days Five and Six (3:27)

**[VISUAL]** Lively illustrations with gentle parallax: a whale breaking the surface; shoals of fish flashing silver; weaver birds building nests; an eagle. Then land animals: an elephant at a waterhole like Mole National Park, goats, a tortoise, a lion, a grasscutter running through the grass. Light sound effects of birds, water and an elephant's trumpet.

**NARRATION**
> On the fifth day, God filled the waters with living creatures and the sky with birds. Suddenly the sea was alive with great whales and flashing fish, and the sky was full of wings, from the tiny weaver bird to the mighty eagle.
>
> Then, on the sixth day, God filled the land with animals. Cattle and goats. Lions and elephants. The tortoise that takes its time, and the grasscutter that never does!
>
> And God saw that it was good.
>
> But God was not finished. The best part of the sixth day was still to come.

---

## Beat 9 · Made in God's image (4:07)

**[VISUAL]** Narrator on camera, leaning forward, voice softer. Then illustration: a man and a woman standing in the morning light, faces full of wonder. Scripture card: **"So God created man in his own image, in the image of God created he him; male and female created he them." · Genesis 1:27 (KJV)**.

**NARRATION**
> Now the Bible slows down, and God says something different: "Let us make man in our image, after our likeness."
>
> "So God created man in his own image, in the image of God created he him; male and female created he them."
>
> Do you hear that? Out of everything in creation, only human beings are made in the image of God. Every person you will ever meet carries something of God's likeness. The president and the street sweeper. You.
>
> God gave the people a job: to fill the earth and to take care of it on His behalf. Then God saw every thing that He had made, "and, behold, it was very good."
>
> Not just good. Very good.

---

## Beat 10 · Dust, breath and a garden (4:57)

**[VISUAL]** Close-up of real hands cupping red-brown soil, letting it run through the fingers. Then illustration: the LORD's presence as warm light bending over a still human form shaped from earth; the man's chest rises as he takes his first breath. Then a lush garden with a river flowing out of it in four directions.

**NARRATION**
> In chapter two, the Bible zooms in, like a camera moving closer, to show us how God made the first man.
>
> "And the LORD God formed man of the dust of the ground, and breathed into his nostrils the breath of life; and man became a living soul."
>
> The same God who flung the stars into space shaped the man from the dust with care, and then gave him breath. The air in your lungs right now is a gift from God.
>
> God planted a garden eastward in Eden, full of trees beautiful to see and good for food. In the middle stood the tree of life, and also the tree of the knowledge of good and evil. God put the man, Adam, in the garden "to dress it and to keep it," to work it and look after it.
>
> Adam could eat from every tree in the garden, except one. God said, "Of the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, thou shalt not eat of it." Keep that tree in your mind. We will come back to it next week.

---

## Beat 11 · Naming the animals, and the first woman (6:14)

**[VISUAL]** Playful illustration: Adam, seated on a rock, as a parade of animals passes by (a giraffe ducking under a branch, a monkey on a deer's back). Then a soft dusk scene: Adam asleep. Then morning: Adam meeting the woman, both full of joy.

**NARRATION**
> Then God said something surprising. Everything so far had been good. But now God said, "It is not good that the man should be alone; I will make him an help meet for him." That means a partner who fits him.
>
> First, God brought the animals to Adam, and Adam named them, one by one. Elephant. Monkey. Giraffe. Goat. But among all those animals, there was no one like him.
>
> So God caused a deep sleep to fall on Adam. He took one of Adam's ribs, and from it He made a woman and brought her to the man.
>
> Adam was overjoyed. He said, "This is now bone of my bones, and flesh of my flesh." The man and the woman lived together with God in the garden, and they were not ashamed. No fear, no hiding, no fighting. Just peace.

---

## Beat 12 · The seventh day (7:13)

**[VISUAL]** Wide, still illustration of the garden at golden hour, everything quiet. Slow, slow zoom. Music bed drops to almost nothing.

**NARRATION**
> And on the seventh day, God rested. Not because He was tired, but because His work was complete. And God blessed the seventh day and made it holy.
>
> This was the world as God made it. Light and life. Work and rest. People and God together, with nothing standing in between.

---

## Beat 13 · Where is Jesus in this story? (7:34)

**[VISUAL]** Narrator on camera. Then scripture cards over the opening image of light breaking into darkness: **John 1:1–3 (KJV)**, then **John 1:14 (KJV)**.

**NARRATION**
> Now you might ask, "Where is Jesus in the story of creation?"
>
> He is right here, from the very first verse. Listen to how the Gospel of John begins, with the same three words as Genesis:
>
> "In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God. The same was in the beginning with God. All things were made by him; and without him was not any thing made that was made."
>
> Who is this Word? John tells us: "And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us." That is Jesus! The One who spoke light into the darkness is the same One who was born in Bethlehem.
>
> And Paul says the God who commanded light to shine out of darkness "hath shined in our hearts, to give the light of the knowledge of the glory of God in the face of Jesus Christ."

---

## Beat 14 · What this teaches us (8:38)

**[VISUAL]** Narrator on camera, with cutaways: a market woman arranging tomatoes; a young man at a sewing machine; a student studying by a lamp during a power cut; children laughing. Text on screen for each point: **1 · You are made on purpose**, **2 · Work is a gift**, **3 · God brings light**.

**NARRATION**
> So, what does this story teach us for everyday life?
>
> **One: you are made on purpose.** You are not an accident. Dark or light, tall or short, from the north or the south, you are made in the image of God. The psalmist says, "I will praise thee; for I am fearfully and wonderfully made." So when someone mocks you, do not believe them. And never look down on anyone else, the house help, the kayayo carrying loads in the market, the new student with the different accent. They carry God's image too.
>
> **Two: work is a gift.** Before anything went wrong in the world, God gave Adam a job. Your work matters to God, whether you are a farmer, a trader, a nurse, a driver or a student writing exams. Do it well, do it honestly, and take care of God's earth. Do not throw rubbish in the gutter. That is God's garden too!
>
> **Three: God brings light into darkness.** We all know what it is like when the lights go off, dumsor, and the house goes black. But the first thing God ever said was, "Let there be light." If your life feels dark right now that same God can speak light into it. Call on Him.
