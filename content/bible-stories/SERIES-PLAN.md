# Bible Stories Series Plan

**Jesus Worldwide · @JesusIsWorldwide**
One narrated story a week for 26 weeks. Each episode goes out twice: as a long-form YouTube video (8–12 minutes) and, with the same narration, as a podcast episode.

## 2. The pitch

*Under the Mango Tree* is a weekly Bible storytelling series from Jesus Worldwide, an Afro gospel ministry in Ghana. Each week a narrator sits in a Ghanaian compound and retells one story from Scripture, faithfully and warmly, the way a grandmother tells stories to the whole house. We move through the Bible's big story in order, from Creation, through Abraham, Moses, David and the prophets, to the birth, death and resurrection of Jesus. Every episode ends by showing how that story points to Christ, gives two or three plain lessons for school, work, the market and home, and closes with a short prayer. It is made for families to watch together, for children at bedtime, for Sunday school teachers looking for something to play, and for adults on the trotro or the commute who want to know the Bible better. It works for every denomination and for the curious. Our own Afro gospel songs open and close each episode, and every episode is also released as a podcast.

## 3. Episode format (8–12 minutes)

|---|---|---|---|

| 2 | **Intro music** | 0:10–0:15 | Series sting cut from one of our songs. Rotate between *Gates of Praise*, *How Great You Are* and *Mysteries of Praise* (instrumental hooks), with the series title card. |
| 3 | **Welcome** | 0:30–0:45 | Narrator on camera under the mango tree: "Akwaaba! Gather round…", the episode number, and one line linking back to last week's story. |
| 4 | **The story** | 5–7 min | The main retelling, in beats. Illustrated stills with slow motion, maps when places matter, and the narrator on camera every few beats. Scripture quotes appear on screen with the reference (KJV or WEB only). |
| 5 | **Where is Jesus in this story?** | 0:45–1:15 | How this story points forward to Christ, using New Testament verses. |
| 6 | **What this teaches us** | 1:30–2:00 | Two or three applications for everyday life in Ghana and around the world: home, school, work, church, the market, online. |
| 7 | **Prayer** | 0:30–0:45 | A short prayer that children can repeat. Put the words on screen. |
| 8 | **Call to action and teaser** | 0:20–0:30 | Subscribe, listen on the podcast, answer a comment question, and a one-line teaser for next week. |
| 9 | **Outro music** | 0:15–0:30 | Song chorus over the end screen (next episode plus subscribe). |

**Scripts:** write every episode in full, with **[VISUAL]** and **NARRATION** for each beat, the way [`ep01-in-the-beginning.md`](ep01-in-the-beginning.md) and [`ep02-the-garden-and-the-promise.md`](ep02-the-garden-and-the-promise.md) are written. Aim for 1,200–1,600 spoken words.

## 5. Production notes for a small team

### Team and weekly workflow

| Role | Who | Weekly time |
|---|---|---|
| Writer | 1 person (can be the narrator) | 4–6 hrs: read the passage in two translations, write the script, check every quote against the KJV or WEB |
| Theology check | Pastor or trusted elder | 30 min: read the script before recording |
| Narrator | A warm, clear human voice | 1–2 hrs: record audio and on-camera links |
| Illustrator | Freelance or volunteer | 8–12 stills per episode (batch 4 episodes at a time) |
| Editor | 1 person | 4–6 hrs: video edit, audio master, thumbnail, upload |

**Run three weeks ahead:** script for week N+3, record for week N+2, edit for week N+1, publish week N.

### Making the video without expensive animation

1. **Illustrated stills (the backbone).** 8–12 paintings or drawings per episode in one consistent style: warm earth tones, our brand colours, and people who look like the world, with African faces and settings shown honestly. Ask a Ghanaian illustrator or art students (KNUST, Ghanatta) to draw at 16:9 and leave space for text. Draw scenes the way the text describes them. Don't depict God the Father as a person. Show His presence with light, a voice, or hands reaching out.
2. **Simple motion.** Slow pans and zooms on each still (the "Ken Burns" effect), parallax by cutting a figure out onto its own layer, slow light leaks, drifting dust or stars, and gentle camera shake for storms. Free tools: **DaVinci Resolve** (free version) or **CapCut** desktop. Make one reusable project template with the title card, lower-thirds, scripture card style and end screen, and copy it every week.
3. **Maps.** A simple animated map whenever a journey matters (Abraham, the Exodus, Jonah, Paul later), with a gold line travelling across it. A Ghana inset ("Nineveh is about as far from Joppa as Accra is from…") helps children feel the distance.
4. **On-camera narrator in a Ghanaian setting.** Film under a mango tree in a compound, or on a wooden bench on a veranda, with kente or an Adinkra-print cloth on the stool, an earthenware pot, and morning or late-afternoon light. Use two angles from one sitting: wide (with 2–4 children listening, with parents' written consent) and a medium close-up. The narrator appears at the welcome, about every 90 seconds during the story, and for the lessons and prayer.
5. **Scripture cards.** Every direct quote appears on screen with its reference and version, e.g. *Genesis 1:1 (KJV)*. Use the same card style every week.
6. **Props and objects.** Close-ups of real things: soil running through fingers (Creation), a fig leaf, a sling and stones, a loaf of bread, a clay lamp. These are cheap, honest and they look good.
7. **Sound.** Our own songs as intro and outro. Soft instrumental beds from our tracks (or properly licensed music) under the story, with the music dropped at key moments so the words land. Light sound effects (wind, water, birds, thunder) from free libraries with a clear licence.
8. **Thumbnails.** One bold illustration, the narrator's face showing the emotion of the story, 2–4 words of text, and the series badge "Bible Stories · Ep 1". Templates go in [`brand/thumbnails.html`](../../brand/thumbnails.html).
9. **Shorts.** Cut one vertical 30–45 s Short per episode (see the table). Reframe the illustration for 9:16, show captions from the first second, and link the full episode with **Related video**.

### Recording the narration

- A quiet room with soft furnishings, or the "blanket fort" trick. Record early in the morning before traffic and generators.
- A USB or XLR dynamic microphone about a hand-span from the mouth, with a pop filter. Record a lavalier mic on camera as a backup.
- Read the script slowly (about 130–150 words a minute), smile on the warm lines, and pause after scripture.
- Clean up in **Audacity** or Resolve's Fairlight page: noise reduction (lightly), EQ, compression. Master to about **−14 LUFS** for the YouTube video and **−16 LUFS** for the podcast file.

### Publishing the audio version

1. **YouTube podcast playlist.** In YouTube Studio, create a playlist called *Under the Mango Tree: Bible Stories*, then go to **Create → New podcast** (or set the existing playlist as a podcast). Add a square podcast cover (at least 1280×1280, 1:1) and a show description. The long-form videos then appear in YouTube Podcasts and YouTube Music.
2. **Spotify for Creators** (the free platform that used to be called Anchor). Create the show, upload each episode's audio master (MP3 or WAV, or the video file if you want a video podcast on Spotify), and add the episode notes: passage, KJV verse, chapter list and a link to the YouTube video. Spotify for Creators gives you an **RSS feed**.
3. **Apple Podcasts and others.** Submit the Spotify for Creators RSS feed once to Apple Podcasts Connect, Amazon Music and other directories. New episodes then appear everywhere automatically.
4. **Write for the ear.** The script must work with the screen off. Don't say "as you can see". Describe what matters ("the sky was black, then suddenly white with light"). For the podcast version, cut any 1–2 second gaps that only made sense with visuals.
5. **Podcast episode title:** `Ep 1 · In the Beginning (Genesis 1–2) | Bible Stories for Families`.

### Audience setting ("Made for Kids")

YouTube requires us to say whether a video is **made for kids**. Videos marked made for kids lose comments, the notification bell, and personalised ads. This series is written for **families and all ages**, with adults as a core audience, so the honest default is **"No, it's not made for kids"**. Keep the presentation family-wide (an adult narrator, adult and child listeners, applications for work and home), not a cartoon aimed only at young children. If we ever make a version clearly aimed at young children (for example a nursery edition), mark that one **made for kids**. When in doubt, follow YouTube's own guidance. Getting this wrong can lead to penalties under children's privacy law (COPPA).

### An honest note on AI

- **AI-assisted illustrations are fine** if we tell people. If a still or animation was made or substantially altered with AI and looks realistic, tick **"Altered or synthetic content"** when uploading (YouTube Studio → Details → *Altered content*: Yes). Also add one line to the description: *"Some illustrations in this video were created with the help of AI tools."* Have a person check every AI image for errors (extra fingers, wrong details, things the Bible doesn't say) and for respectful depictions.
- **The narration must be a real human voice.** No AI voice clones or text-to-speech narrator. A real person telling the story is the heart of this series. It's what makes it trustworthy and warm, and it keeps us clear of YouTube's rules on synthetic media.
- **Every episode must be written for real, not templated.** YouTube's monetisation policy on **inauthentic (repetitious or mass-produced) content** penalises channels that publish near-identical videos with the words swapped. The *format* repeats every week, and that's fine. But the script, the story beats, the applications, the prayer and the illustrations must be written fresh for each passage by a person who has read the passage. AI can help with research, outlines or proofreading, but a person writes and owns the final script, and every scripture quote is checked word for word against the KJV or WEB.

### Scripture and content rules (every episode)

- Retell faithfully. Light dramatisation (sounds, feelings, setting) is fine if it is plainly consistent with the text. Never put words in a character's mouth that contradict Scripture, and quote Scripture exactly when you quote it.
- Direct quotes **only from the KJV or WEB** (both public domain), with the reference on screen and in the description.
- Christ-centred: every episode includes "Where is Jesus in this story?"
- Avoid denominational debates (for example the age of the earth, the mode of baptism, Sabbath versus Sunday, spiritual gifts, end-times timelines). Tell what the text says and leave the debates to local churches.
- Hashtags: **#BibleStories #JesusWorldwide** plus story-specific ones, up to 7 in a description. **Never #nsppd or #elroi.**
- Pinned comment: the episode's key verse plus "Where are you listening from? 🌍"
