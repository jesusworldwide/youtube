# Episode 2 · The Garden, the Lie and the Promise

**Series:** Under the Mango Tree: Bible Stories
**Passage:** Genesis 3:1–24 (with Galatians 4:4; 1 John 3:8; Luke 19:10; 1 John 1:9; Revelation 12:9; 22:2)
**Runtime target:** about 11½ minutes (narration 1,590 words at about 150 words a minute, plus music)
**Scripture version for on-screen quotes:** King James Version (KJV), public domain
**Intro / outro music:** *Mysteries of Praise* (instrumental hook for the intro, chorus for the outro)

> **How to read this script:** each beat has a **[VISUAL]** line (what's on screen) and **NARRATION** (the exact words to read; the lines beginning with ">"). Scripture in the narration is quoted word for word from the KJV. Read it slowly and show it on a scripture card. For the podcast version the narration stands alone. Nothing depends on seeing the screen.
>
> **Tone note:** this is a sad story with a hopeful ending. Keep the serpent unsettling but not scary for young children. No horror music, no monster imagery. The serpent is shown as a beautiful, gleaming snake in the branches, mostly in shadow.

## Beat 3 · Welcome and recap (0:28)

**[VISUAL]** Narrator on camera under the mango tree, morning light this time. The same children on the mat, plus one or two new faces. Quick cutaways to last week's illustrations: light breaking, the stars, Adam and Eve in the garden.

**NARRATION**
> Akwaaba, and welcome back under the mango tree! If you are new, come and sit. Episode one is linked right here, and on the podcast too.
>
> Last week we saw God create everything, and then people, a man and a woman, made in God's own image. God put them in a garden called Eden. Everything was "very good." No sickness, no fear, no fighting. And God walked with them.
>
> But there was one tree in the middle of the garden, the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, and God had said, "Of the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, thou shalt not eat of it: for in the day that thou eatest thereof thou shalt surely die."
>
> So what happened next? Genesis, chapter three.

---

## Beat 4 · The serpent's question (1:21)

**[VISUAL]** Illustration: the garden in full colour, the woman standing near the great tree. In the branches, a long serpent with gleaming scales, half in shadow. Scripture card: **"Yea, hath God said, Ye shall not eat of every tree of the garden?" · Genesis 3:1 (KJV)**.

**NARRATION**
> "Now the serpent was more subtil than any beast of the field which the LORD God had made."
>
> Subtil means crafty, clever in a sneaky way. The book of Revelation tells us who this really was: "that old serpent, called the Devil, and Satan." God's enemy, who wanted to spoil God's beautiful world.
>
> And notice how he starts. Not with a loud lie, but with a question. He said to the woman, "Yea, hath God said, Ye shall not eat of every tree of the garden?"
>
> God had given them every tree but one! But the serpent made it sound as if God was stingy, holding back good things from them.

---

## Beat 5 · The woman's answer (2:08)

**[VISUAL]** Close-up illustration: the woman's face, thoughtful, a little unsure. Her hand gestures towards the many fruit trees around her.

**NARRATION**
> The woman answered, "We may eat of the fruit of the trees of the garden: But of the fruit of the tree which is in the midst of the garden, God hath said, Ye shall not eat of it, neither shall ye touch it, lest ye die."
>
> She knew God's command. But she was talking with the enemy instead of walking away. And that is always dangerous.

---

## Beat 6 · The lie (2:36)

**[VISUAL]** The serpent moves closer, coiled around the branch beside the fruit. The light on the scene grows slightly colder. Scripture card: **"Ye shall not surely die" · Genesis 3:4 (KJV)**.

**NARRATION**
> Then the serpent stopped asking questions and told a straight lie.
>
> "Ye shall not surely die: For God doth know that in the day ye eat thereof, then your eyes shall be opened, and ye shall be as gods, knowing good and evil."
>
> Listen carefully. God had said, "thou shalt surely die." The serpent said, "Ye shall not surely die." He took God's own words and put a "not" in the middle. And then he made God sound like someone who was afraid of them, someone keeping the best for Himself.
>
> Temptation still works like that. It whispers, "Did God really say that? Just this once. Nobody will know."

---

## Beat 7 · The choice (3:22)

**[VISUAL]** Slow sequence: the woman looking up at the fruit; her hand reaching; the fruit in her hand; then her turning to the man, who has been standing with her. The camera holds on the bitten fruit. Music bed drops to a single low note.

**NARRATION**
> The woman looked at the tree again. And now it looked different. The Bible says she "saw that the tree was good for food, and that it was pleasant to the eyes, and a tree to be desired to make one wise."
>
> So "she took of the fruit thereof, and did eat, and gave also unto her husband with her; and he did eat."
>
> Adam was right there with her. He did not stop her. He simply took it, and ate.
>
> *(pause)*
>
> And in that moment, everything changed.

---

## Beat 8 · Fig leaves and hiding (3:59)

**[VISUAL]** Illustration: the man and woman, suddenly ashamed, hurriedly tying fig leaves together. Real close-up insert: a hand pulling a large leaf from a branch. Then: evening light, the two of them crouched behind the trees, peeking out.

**NARRATION**
> The serpent had promised their eyes would be opened. And they were, but not the way they hoped. "And the eyes of them both were opened, and they knew that they were naked; and they sewed fig leaves together, and made themselves aprons."
>
> For the first time ever, they felt shame.
>
> Then, in the cool of the day, they heard the voice of the LORD God walking in the garden. Before, that sound would have made them run to meet Him. Now they ran the other way and hid themselves among the trees.
>
> Can you imagine? Hiding from the God who made the trees!

---

## Beat 9 · "Where art thou?" (4:42)

**[VISUAL]** Narrator on camera, speaking gently. Then illustration: warm light (the presence of the LORD) moving through the garden, the trees in silhouette. Scripture card, large and slow: **"Where art thou?" · Genesis 3:9 (KJV)**.

**NARRATION**
> And then God called out. "And the LORD God called unto Adam, and said unto him, Where art thou?"
>
> Now children, did God not know where Adam was? Of course He knew! He was giving Adam a chance to come out and tell the truth. Like a father who knows exactly who broke the window, but still asks, "What happened here?"
>
> Adam said, "I heard thy voice in the garden, and I was afraid, because I was naked; and I hid myself."
>
> God said, "Who told thee that thou wast naked? Hast thou eaten of the tree, whereof I commanded thee that thou shouldest not eat?"

---

## Beat 10 · The blame game (5:27)

**[VISUAL]** Illustration in two panels: Adam pointing at the woman; the woman pointing at the serpent. The serpent slipping away into the grass.

**NARRATION**
> And here comes the blame game.
>
> Adam said, "The woman whom thou gavest to be with me, she gave me of the tree, and I did eat." Did you catch that? He blamed his wife, and he even hinted that it was God's fault for giving her to him.
>
> Then God said to the woman, "What is this that thou hast done?" And the woman said, "The serpent beguiled me, and I did eat."
>
> The man blamed the woman. The woman blamed the serpent. And nobody said, "I am sorry. It was me."

---

## Beat 11 · The consequences, and the promise (6:06)

**[VISUAL]** Narrator on camera, serious. Then illustration: the serpent on the ground in the dust. Then a striking image: a heel coming down on the serpent's head, shown as a stylised silhouette against a sunrise. Scripture card: **"…it shall bruise thy head, and thou shalt bruise his heel." · Genesis 3:15 (KJV)**.

**NARRATION**
> Sin always brings consequences. To the serpent, God said it would crawl on its belly in the dust. And then God said something we must not miss:
>
> "And I will put enmity between thee and the woman, and between thy seed and her seed; it shall bruise thy head, and thou shalt bruise his heel."
>
> This is the very first promise of a Saviour in the Bible. One day a child would be born from the woman's line. The serpent would hurt Him, bruise His heel. But that child would crush the serpent's head.
>
> Then God told the woman that bearing children would be painful. And He told Adam that the ground was cursed, that work would be hard, with thorns and thistles and sweat, and that one day he would return to the dust.
>
> Death had entered God's good world.

---

## Beat 12 · Clothes of skins, and the closed gate (7:05)

**[VISUAL]** Illustration: tender scene, light bending over Adam and Eve as they are clothed in garments of animal skin. Then: the two walking out of the garden, looking back. At the east of the garden, cherubim and a flaming sword turning every way, shown as brilliant light, not frightening.

**NARRATION**
> But even here, in the saddest part of the story, look at God's kindness. Adam named his wife Eve, because she would be the mother of all living. And "unto Adam also and to his wife did the LORD God make coats of skins, and clothed them."
>
> Their fig leaves were not enough. So God Himself covered them.
>
> Then God sent them out of the garden, so that they would not eat from the tree of life and live forever in their broken state. At the east of the garden He placed cherubim, mighty angels, and a flaming sword, to guard the way to the tree of life.
>
> The gate to the garden was closed. But God's promise was open.

---

## Beat 13 · Where is Jesus in this story? (7:56)

**[VISUAL]** Narrator on camera. Then a slow montage of quick sketches looking forward: a manger, a carpenter's workshop, a cross on a hill at dusk, an empty tomb at sunrise. Scripture cards for each verse.

**NARRATION**
> So, who is the child who would crush the serpent's head? The apostle Paul tells us: "But when the fulness of the time was come, God sent forth his Son, made of a woman."
>
> That is Jesus! On the cross the enemy bruised His heel. Jesus suffered and died. But on the third day He rose again, and the serpent's head was crushed. The Bible says, "For this purpose the Son of God was manifested, that he might destroy the works of the devil."
>
> And remember the tree of life that was guarded? At the very end of the Bible, the tree of life appears again, in God's new city, and "the leaves of the tree were for the healing of the nations." What was closed in Genesis is opened through Jesus. For every nation, including yours.

---

## Beat 14 · What this teaches us (8:53)

**[VISUAL]** Narrator on camera, with cutaways: a phone screen full of forwarded messages; an open Bible on a table; a child coming to a parent with a broken plate; a mother hugging the child. Text on screen: **1 · Know what God really said**, **2 · Don't hide, don't blame**, **3 · God comes looking for you**.

**NARRATION**
> So what does this story teach us for everyday life?
>
> **One: know what God really said.** The serpent won by twisting God's word, adding a little, taking away a little. Today there are many voices: rumours, WhatsApp forwards, even people who say "the Bible says" when it doesn't. The best protection is to read the Bible for yourself.
>
> **Two: don't hide, don't blame.** When we do wrong, we want to cover it up, or blame our spouse, our parents, our boss, even the devil. But the way back to God is honesty. "If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness." Parents, let your children see you say sorry too.
>
> **Three: God comes looking for you.** Adam and Eve hid, but God came walking into the garden calling, "Where art thou?" Jesus said, "For the Son of man is come to seek and to save that which was lost." Maybe you feel far from God today, ashamed of something you have done. He is calling your name. Come out from behind the trees.
