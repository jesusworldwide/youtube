# The Worship Life, Episode 01: Enter With Thanks (Psalm 100)

**Series:** The Worship Life
**Passage:** Psalm 100 (KJV)
**Linked song:** "Gates of Praise", Jesus Worldwide
**Target length:** about 15 to 18 minutes (script body about 2,400 words at a calm teaching pace)
**Big idea:** Thanksgiving is the God-given doorway into His presence, and every believer can walk through it today, whatever they are carrying.

> Stage directions are in *[brackets and italics]*. Scripture appears on screen with reference and version.

## SCRIPT

### 1. Hook (0:00 to 0:15)

*[Close shot. Teacher looks straight into the camera. No music yet.]*

Every gate has a key. Some gates need a padlock key, some need a gateman, some need a password. But the gate into the presence of God? The Bible says the key is already in your mouth. Let me show you.

### 2. Welcome (0:15 to 0:45)

*[Soft instrumental of "Gates of Praise" fades in under the title card, then out. Wide shot.]*

Welcome to The Worship Life. I'm so glad you're here. Whether you are watching from Accra or Kumasi, from Lagos, Lomé, London or Houston, you are welcome. This series is about one simple thing: learning to live a life of worship, not only on Sunday, but every day of the week. Today we are starting at the front door, with a short psalm that has carried God's people for three thousand years: Psalm 100. Get your Bible, get a pen, and let's go.

### 3. Story: The gate in Adenta (0:45 to 2:45)

*[Medium shot. Relaxed storytelling tone.]*

Some years ago I went to visit an old friend in Adenta. I had the address, I had the directions, and after about forty minutes in traffic I found the house. Big wall, big black metal gate. I knocked. Nothing. I knocked again. I could hear a radio inside and a dog barking, but nobody came. So I started doing what we all do. I called his phone. No answer. I stood outside in the sun, sweating, getting a little bit frustrated, thinking, "Maybe I should just go home."

Then a small boy came along with a bucket on his head and looked at me standing there. He said, "Uncle, you won't get in like that. You have to call Auntie's name." So I shouted, "Auntie Adwoa!" And immediately the small door in the big gate opened, and there was my friend's mother, smiling. "Ah, you have come! Come in, come in, you are welcome!"

I had the right house. I had the right address. I was even standing at the right gate. But I did not know how that house was entered. Once I used the right word, the gate opened, and I was not just let in; I was welcomed.

Many believers live like I stood outside that gate. We know God is real. We believe in Jesus. We come to church. But inside, we feel like we are standing outside in the sun, knocking, calling, waiting, wondering why we do not sense His nearness. And Psalm 100 lovingly tells us how God's house is entered. It tells us the word that opens the gate. Let's read it together.

### 4. The passage read aloud (2:45 to 4:00)

*[Teacher opens the Bible and reads slowly. Each verse appears on screen.]*

Psalm 100, from the King James Version. It has a little heading: "A Psalm of praise."

> "Make a joyful noise unto the LORD, all ye lands.
> Serve the LORD with gladness: come before his presence with singing.
> Know ye that the LORD he is God: it is he that hath made us, and not we ourselves; we are his people, and the sheep of his pasture.
> Enter into his gates with thanksgiving, and into his courts with praise: be thankful unto him, and bless his name.
> For the LORD is good; his mercy is everlasting; and his truth endureth to all generations."
> (Psalm 100:1 to 5, KJV)

*[Pause. Close the Bible halfway.]*

Five verses. You could read it in thirty seconds. But inside these five verses is a whole school of worship. I want to give you three things from this psalm today, and for each one I want to show you how to live it on a normal Tuesday, not just a Sunday.

### 5. Point 1: Thanksgiving is an invitation for everyone (4:00 to 7:00)

*[On screen: "1. An invitation for everyone (v. 1 to 2)"]*

Look at how the psalm begins: "Make a joyful noise unto the LORD, all ye lands." Not "all ye priests." Not "all ye choir members." Not "all ye people who have their lives sorted out." All ye lands. All the earth.

Now remember who first sang this. Israel, a small nation in the Middle East. But their songbook was already looking past their borders, calling every land, every nation, every people to come and worship the Lord. Thousands of years before anyone heard of Ghana, God's invitation already had our name in it. That is why we call this ministry Jesus Worldwide. The invitation to praise is not local; it is global.

And notice the mood of the invitation. "A joyful noise." "Gladness." "Singing." God is not inviting us to a funeral; He is inviting us to a celebration. Some of us grew up thinking God is happiest when we are serious and quiet and a little bit afraid. Reverence is good and right, and we will talk about it in this series. But Psalm 100 shows us that God also delights in His people's gladness. The word "serve" here is the language of a servant or a worker, yet the psalm says, serve Him with gladness. Our service is not forced labour. It is the glad work of children in their Father's house.

**Application.** So here is the first practical step. Thanksgiving is for you, right where you are. You don't have to wait until you feel holy enough or until things improve. If you can breathe, you are invited. Psalm 150 says, "Let every thing that hath breath praise the LORD."

This week, try this. When you wake up, before you pick up your phone, before you check WhatsApp, say one sentence of thanks out loud. Not in your head; out loud, even quietly. "Lord, thank You for this new day." It sounds small. But you are training your mouth to go to God first. A joyful noise does not have to be loud. It just has to be real.

### 6. Point 2: Thanksgiving begins with knowing who God is and whose we are (7:00 to 11:00)

*[On screen: "2. Know who He is, and whose you are (v. 3)"]*

Verse 3 is the centre of the psalm, and it is a teaching verse: "Know ye that the LORD he is God: it is he that hath made us, and not we ourselves; we are his people, and the sheep of his pasture."

Real thanksgiving is not just a mood. It comes out of knowledge. The psalm says, know this. Know three things.

First, know that the LORD, He is God. Not money, not your boss, not the government, not your own strength. When we forget that the Lord is God, we start to bow down to things that cannot help us. And whatever we think is God, that is what we will thank. If we think our hustle is god, we will thank our hustle. If we know the Lord is God, our thanks goes up to Him.

Second, know that He made us, and not we ourselves. We live in a world that loves the phrase "self-made." We put it on t-shirts and Instagram bios. But Psalm 100 says gently, "No, my friend, you are not self-made." You did not choose the family you were born into. You did not design your own heart to beat. Every gift you have, every skill, every breath, came from His hand. And that is not bad news; it is good news. If you made yourself, then everything depends on you. But if God made you, then you belong to Someone who knows exactly how you work and what you need.

Third, know that we are His people and the sheep of His pasture. In Ghana we know what it means to see a herdsman walking with cattle or sheep through the bush in the north. Those animals are not wandering alone. Someone is watching them, leading them to water and grass, chasing away danger. The psalm says that is who you are to God. You are not a stray. You are cared for.

And for us who believe in Jesus, this picture comes even closer. Jesus said, "I am the good shepherd: the good shepherd giveth his life for the sheep" (John 10:11). The Shepherd of Psalm 100 has a name and a face. He went to the cross for His sheep and rose again. When you give thanks, you are not shouting into the sky. You are speaking to the Shepherd who laid down His life for you.

**Application.** So how do we live this? Here is the practical step: thank God for who He is before you thank Him for what He gave. Many of our prayers are lists. "Thank You for the job, thank You for the car, thank You for the visa." It is good to thank Him for those things. But if the job disappears, does the thanksgiving disappear too?

This week, when you pray, start with one sentence about who God is. "Lord, You are my Maker." "Lord, You are my Shepherd." "Lord, You are good." Then move to what He has done. You will find that your thanksgiving becomes stronger, because it is built on Him, not only on your circumstances.

And one more thing. When you are tempted to think, "I did this by myself," stop and say, "Lord, You made me, and not I myself." Humility and thanksgiving are close friends.

### 7. Point 3: Thanksgiving rests on God's unchanging character (11:00 to 14:30)

*[On screen: "3. He is good, merciful and faithful (v. 4 to 5)"]*

Now we come to the gate itself. Verse 4: "Enter into his gates with thanksgiving, and into his courts with praise: be thankful unto him, and bless his name."

In ancient Jerusalem, the temple had gates and outer courts. When God's people came up to worship, they passed through the gates, into the courts, toward the place where God had promised to meet them. And the psalm says: this is how you come in. Through the gate of thanksgiving, into the courts of praise.

Do you see it? Thanksgiving is the word at the gate. It is like my "Auntie Adwoa." It is the way God's house is entered. Not complaining, not demanding, not bargaining. Thanksgiving.

And today, through Jesus, we have something even greater than the temple gates. The book of Hebrews tells us that by the blood of Jesus we can come boldly into the very presence of God (Hebrews 10:19). Jesus Himself is the way in. So when we give thanks, we are not earning our way through the gate. Jesus has opened it. Thanksgiving is simply how we walk in, with the right heart.

But then, why should we be thankful? Because life is always easy? No. Verse 5 gives us the reason, and it is not about our situation at all. "For the LORD is good; his mercy is everlasting; and his truth endureth to all generations."

Three unchanging things. He is good. His mercy is everlasting. His truth, His faithfulness, endures to all generations.

Your situation changes. His character does not. Prices go up at the market. Light goes off. Health goes up and down. Jobs come and go. Relationships disappoint us. But the Lord is good on your best day and good on your worst day. His mercy did not expire last year. His faithfulness to your grandmother is the same faithfulness that holds you now, and it will hold your children.

I need to be honest with you. Psalm 100 does not promise that if you give thanks, all your problems will disappear. That is not what it says. The Bible says, "In every thing give thanks: for this is the will of God in Christ Jesus concerning you" (1 Thessalonians 5:18). In everything, not necessarily for everything. You don't have to thank God for the sickness or the betrayal. But in the middle of it, you can thank Him that He is still good, His mercy is still everlasting, and He has not left you.

**Application.** So here is the third practical step. When life is hard, use verse 5 as your thanksgiving prayer. Say it out loud: "Lord, You are good. Your mercy is everlasting. Your faithfulness endures." Say it in the hospital. Say it at the bus stop. Say it when the exam result is not what you hoped for. It will not deny the pain, but it will lift your eyes.

And here is a challenge for the week: keep a thanksgiving list. Every night before you sleep, write down three specific things you can thank God for from that day. Not general things, specific things. "The trotro driver who gave me back my change." "My daughter's laugh." "The rain that cooled the evening." By the end of the week you will have twenty-one reasons. And you will start to see what was always true: God's goodness was all around you, you just needed eyes to see it.

### 8. Response and prayer (14:30 to 16:15)

*[Slow push-in to close shot. Soft pad or acoustic guitar under, very low.]*

Before we close, I want to give you a moment to walk through that gate right now. Maybe you have been standing outside for a long time. Maybe you have been carrying disappointment, or worry, or grief, and you have not thanked God in a long time. That's okay. The gate is open. Jesus has opened it.

And maybe you have never come to God through Jesus at all. Psalm 100 says, "We are his people, and the sheep of his pasture." You can become one of His sheep today. Jesus died for your sins and rose again, and He invites you to come to Him by faith.

Let's pray together. You can pray this out loud with me.

*[Teacher closes eyes, speaks slowly.]*

"Lord God, I come to You through Jesus Christ. Thank You that You are God, and there is no other. Thank You that You made me, and I am not my own. Thank You that I am Your sheep, and You are my Shepherd. Jesus, thank You for laying down Your life for me and rising again. Forgive me for the times I have complained instead of giving thanks, and for the times I have forgotten You. Today I enter Your gates with thanksgiving. You are good. Your mercy is everlasting. Your faithfulness endures to all generations. Teach me to live a life of worship, every day, in every season. In Jesus' name, Amen."

If you prayed that prayer and gave your life to Jesus for the first time today, please tell us in the comments, or send us a message. We would love to pray for you and help you take the next step, including finding a Bible-believing church near you.

### 9. Call to action and song introduction (16:15 to 17:30)

*[Medium shot, warm and upbeat.]*

Here is your homework for this week. Three things. One: say thanks out loud before you touch your phone in the morning. Two: start your prayers with who God is. Three: write down three specific thanks every night. Then come back next week and tell us in the comments what you noticed.

If this teaching blessed you, subscribe to Jesus Worldwide and turn on notifications, because every week in The Worship Life we will open another passage on worship. Next week, we go to Psalm 103: "Forget not all his benefits." And share this video with one person who needs to walk through the gate of thanksgiving this week.

Now, as we close, I want to leave you with a song. When we read Psalm 100, our hearts were stirred to write it. It is called "Gates of Praise." It takes the words of this psalm, "enter into His gates with thanksgiving," and turns them into a song you can sing all week: in the car, in the kitchen, at your desk. The chorus says, "I will give You thanks in every season, I will give You praise for every reason." That is exactly what we have been learning today.

So don't just listen. Sing it. Make a joyful noise unto the Lord. Let's enter His gates together.
