# Enter His Gates

## 30 Days of Thanksgiving and Praise

*A devotional from Jesus Worldwide*

> "Enter into his gates with thanksgiving, and into his courts with praise: be thankful unto him, and bless his name."
> (Psalm 100:4, KJV)

*Scripture quotations are taken from the King James Version of the Bible, which is in the public domain.*

---

## Introduction

There is a gate in front of every one of us. Some mornings it feels wide open. Other mornings it feels locked, heavy and far away. We know God is there. We believe in Jesus. But somewhere between the worry about school fees, the pressure at work, the light that went off again and the news on our phones, we lose our way into His presence.

This little book is about the way in.

Three thousand years ago, God gave His people a short song that we now call Psalm 100. It tells us how God's house is entered: "Enter into his gates with thanksgiving, and into his courts with praise." Thanksgiving is the gate. Praise is the court. And through Jesus Christ, who opened the way by His cross and resurrection, that gate stands open to anyone who will come.

At Jesus Worldwide, we are a music ministry from Ghana. Our mandate is to proclaim the Good News of Jesus Christ through music, video and every kind of media. When we read Psalm 100, it became a song, "Gates of Praise." But we realised that a song lasts four minutes, and thanksgiving is meant to last a lifetime. So we wrote this devotional to help you walk through the gate every day for thirty days, until it becomes the natural path of your heart.

Let us be clear about what this book is not. It is not a formula for getting things from God. Thanksgiving is not a key that forces heaven to give us what we want. The Bible never promises that a grateful person will never suffer, never lack and never cry. What it does promise is far better: that the Lord is good, that His mercy is everlasting, and that His faithfulness endures to all generations (Psalm 100:5). Thanksgiving does not change who God is. It changes us, by opening our eyes to who He has always been.

Over the next thirty days we will move through four stages. In the first week we stand at the gate and learn the basics of thanksgiving. In the second we learn to give thanks in hard seasons, following the example of Job, Daniel, Paul and Silas, and above all Jesus, who gave thanks on the night He was betrayed. In the third week we step into the courts of praise and fix our eyes on the greatness of God. In the final week we see how worship becomes a whole way of life, ending with a picture of every nation, Ghana included, singing before the throne of the Lamb.

Wherever you are reading from, Accra or Abidjan, Kumasi or Kansas, a hospital bed or a busy office, you are welcome. Come in. The gate is open.

## How to Use This Book

1. **Give it ten to fifteen minutes a day.** Each day is short enough to read in one sitting. Morning is best, before your phone and your to-do list take over, but any regular time works.

2. **Read the scripture slowly, out loud if you can.** Every day begins with a passage from the King James Version. Read it twice. Underline a word or phrase that catches your attention.

3. **Read the reflection and the illustration.** These are written to connect the Bible with ordinary life: the market, the office, the classroom, the family. The people in the illustrations are composites drawn from everyday life; their names have been changed or created.

4. **Pray this.** Each day ends with a short prayer. Pray it as written, or let it start you off and continue in your own words and your own language.

5. **Live it today.** One simple, practical action. Don't skip this. Thanksgiving grows by practice, not just by reading.

6. **Journal.** Keep a notebook, or use the notes app on your phone. Answer the journal question honestly. By Day 30 you will have a record of God's goodness you can return to for years.

7. **Use it with others.** This devotional works well for families, youth groups, church cell groups and WhatsApp prayer groups. Read the day on your own, then share your journal answer with the group.

8. **Listen along.** Each week links to a Jesus Worldwide song and to our weekly teaching series, *The Worship Life*, on YouTube (@JesusIsWorldwide). Let the music carry the scriptures into the rest of your day.

9. **If you miss a day, simply continue.** This is not a test. God's mercies are new every morning, and so is your next page.

## Day 1: Enter His Gates

> "Enter into his gates with thanksgiving, and into his courts with praise: be thankful unto him, and bless his name."
> (Psalm 100:4, KJV)

### Reflection

Every house has a way in. In Ghana, many homes are built behind walls with a metal gate at the front. You can have the right address and still stand outside in the sun if you do not know how that house is entered. Sometimes there is a bell. Sometimes you must call a name. Sometimes the gateman must recognise your face.

Psalm 100 tells us how God's house is entered. When the people of Israel went up to worship at the temple in Jerusalem, they passed through gates into open courtyards, moving toward the place where God had promised to meet them. The psalm gives them, and us, the word at the gate: thanksgiving. Not complaint. Not bargaining. Not a list of demands. Thanks.

This is not because God is a proud king who needs flattery before He will listen. It is because thanksgiving puts our hearts in the right place. It reminds us that we are coming to Someone who has already been good to us. It turns our eyes away from ourselves and toward Him.

For followers of Jesus, there is something even greater. The temple gates have long gone, but Hebrews 10:19 tells us that we can enter the very presence of God "by the blood of Jesus." Jesus is the true gate. He has opened the way by His death and resurrection. We do not earn our way in by being thankful enough. Thanksgiving is simply how a forgiven child walks into the Father's house: with gladness, with gratitude, with a song.

### Illustration

Akosua is a nurse in Kumasi. She works long night shifts, and most mornings she gets home tired and anxious, her mind full of patients and paperwork. For years her morning prayers sounded the same: "Lord, help me. Lord, fix this. Lord, why?" She was praying, but she felt far away.

One morning, too tired even to make a list, she simply sat on the edge of her bed and whispered, "Thank You, Lord, that I got home safely. Thank You for the patient who recovered. Thank You that You were with me all night." She was surprised by what happened. Nothing around her changed, but something inside her did. For the first time in weeks she felt that she was not knocking on a closed gate. She was being welcomed home. Her needs were still real, and she still brought them to God. But now she brought them from inside the gate, not from outside it.

### Pray this

"Father, thank You that through Jesus the gate into Your presence is open to me. I don't come because I am good enough; I come because You are good. Today I choose to enter with thanksgiving. Thank You for life, for breath and for Your love. Teach me to walk into Your presence every day with a grateful heart. In Jesus' name, Amen."

### Live it today

Before you pick up your phone this morning, say one sentence of thanks to God out loud. Then, at three points in the day (morning, midday, evening), stop for ten seconds and thank Him for one specific thing.

### Journal question

When you come to God in prayer, what usually comes out of your mouth first? What would change if you began with thanksgiving?

## Day 2: You Are Not Self-Made

> "Know ye that the LORD he is God: it is he that hath made us, and not we ourselves; we are his people, and the sheep of his pasture."
> (Psalm 100:3, KJV)

### Reflection

Before Psalm 100 tells us to enter God's gates, it tells us to know something. Real thanksgiving is not a mood we work up; it grows out of truth. This verse gives us three truths to stand on.

First, "the LORD he is God." Not money, not our employer, not our own cleverness. Whatever we believe is in charge of our lives, that is what we will end up thanking. If we think our hustle is god, we will thank our hustle. If we know the Lord is God, our thanks rises to Him.

Second, "it is he that hath made us, and not we ourselves." Our world loves the word "self-made." It sounds strong and independent. But none of us chose the day we were born, the family we came from, or the heart that beats in our chest without our permission. Every gift, every talent, every opportunity, every breath came from His hand.

This could sound like bad news, but it is the best news. If you made yourself, everything depends on you, and you must hold yourself together alone. But if God made you, you belong to Someone who understands exactly how you are made and what you need.

Third, "we are his people, and the sheep of his pasture." Sheep need a shepherd who leads them to grass and water and protects them from danger. That is who God is to His people. Jesus took this picture and put His own name on it: "I am the good shepherd: the good shepherd giveth his life for the sheep" (John 10:11). The Shepherd of Psalm 100 went to the cross for His sheep.

### Illustration

Kwame grew up in a small town in the Volta Region. Through years of study and hard work, he became a successful engineer in Accra. At his company's end-of-year party, a colleague praised him: "Kwame, you are a self-made man!" Everyone clapped, and Kwame smiled.

But that night, driving home, he remembered his mother selling kenkey by the roadside to pay his fees. He remembered the teacher who gave him extra lessons for free, and the uncle who let him sleep in his room in Madina for two years. He remembered the night he nearly died of malaria and recovered. He pulled his car to the side of the road and wept. "Lord," he said, "I am not self-made. You made me, and You sent people to carry me. Thank You." From that day his gratitude became deeper, and so did his humility.

### Pray this

"Lord, You are God, and there is no other. You made me, and I did not make myself. Thank You for every gift, every person and every breath You have given me. Thank You, Jesus, for being my Good Shepherd, who laid down His life for me. Keep me humble and grateful, knowing that I belong to You. Amen."

### Live it today

Write down the names of three people God used to carry you to where you are today. Thank God for each of them by name, then send at least one of them a message of thanks.

### Journal question

In what area of your life are you most tempted to think, "I did this by myself"? How does knowing that God made you change the way you see it?

## Day 3: Forget Not

> "Bless the LORD, O my soul: and all that is within me, bless his holy name. Bless the LORD, O my soul, and forget not all his benefits."
> (Psalm 103:1 to 2, KJV)

### Reflection

Have you noticed that David is talking to himself in this psalm? "Bless the LORD, O my soul." He is not waiting for his feelings to lead him into worship. He is leading his feelings. He is preaching to his own heart.

Sometimes that is exactly what we must do. Our souls are not always ready to praise. Some mornings they are heavy, distracted or discouraged. David shows us that worship is often a decision before it is a feeling. We can say to ourselves, "Soul, wake up. Bless the Lord."

Then he names the great enemy of gratitude: forgetfulness. "Forget not all his benefits." Human beings forget quickly. Israel saw the Red Sea open, and within days they were complaining about water. We are no different. The prayer God answered last year becomes old news. The healing, the provision, the protection fade from memory, and we begin to live as though God has never helped us.

So David counts. In the next verses he lists God's benefits: He forgives all our iniquities, heals our diseases, redeems our life from destruction, crowns us with lovingkindness and tender mercies, and satisfies our mouth with good things (Psalm 103:3 to 5).

Notice what comes first: forgiveness. Before any other benefit, God deals with our sin. For us who live after the cross, this benefit has a name: Jesus Christ, who "his own self bare our sins in his own body on the tree" (1 Peter 2:24). If God never gave us anything else, the forgiveness we have in Jesus would be reason enough to praise Him forever.

Remembering is not just looking backward. It builds faith for today. The God who helped you before is the same God who stands with you now.

### Illustration

Every December, Auntie Efua sits at her dining table in Takoradi with an old exercise book. On the cover she has written, "What God Has Done." Inside are thirty years of entries in her careful handwriting: the year her son passed his exams, the year the doctors found the lump was not cancer, the year she lost her job and the year a neighbour quietly paid her rent. There are hard entries too: the year her husband died, and under it, "The Lord carried me through."

When her grandchildren complain, she sometimes brings out the book and reads to them. "You see," she tells them, "God has never failed this family. Not once. He did not always do what we asked. But He never left us." Her book is her Psalm 103.

### Pray this

"Lord, I bless Your holy name with all that is within me. Forgive me for how quickly I forget Your goodness. Thank You, above all, for forgiving my sins through Jesus. Thank You for every time You have helped, protected, provided for and comforted me. Help me to remember, so that I can trust You today. Amen."

### Live it today

Start your own "What God Has Done" list. Write down at least ten specific things God has done for you, beginning with your salvation. Keep the list somewhere you will see it again.

### Journal question

What is one thing God did for you in the past that you have almost forgotten? How does remembering it encourage you for what you are facing now?

## Day 4: New Every Morning

> "It is of the LORD's mercies that we are not consumed, because his compassions fail not. They are new every morning: great is thy faithfulness."
> (Lamentations 3:22 to 23, KJV)

### Reflection

These beautiful words were not written in a time of peace. The book of Lamentations is a funeral song. Jerusalem had been destroyed, the temple burned and the people carried away. The writer has spent two and a half chapters weeping. He describes himself as a man who has "seen affliction" (Lamentations 3:1). He does not hide his pain.

And then, right in the middle of the ruins, he says, "This I recall to my mind, therefore have I hope" (Lamentations 3:21). He chooses to remember something. He remembers that God's mercy has not run out. He is still alive. He has not been consumed. And that, too, is God's mercy.

"They are new every morning." God's compassion is not like yesterday's food warmed up again. Each day He gives fresh mercy, enough for that day. We do not need to worry whether God's mercy will last for next month's problems. When next month comes, there will be new mercy waiting.

This means thanksgiving is not only for big moments. It is for ordinary mornings. Waking up is a mercy. Breath is a mercy. A clear mind, a cup of tea, a phone call from a friend, the sound of children playing outside: all mercies. The person who learns to notice morning mercies will find reasons to give thanks every single day.

It also means that yesterday's failure does not have the final word. If you fell short yesterday, God's mercy is new this morning. Through Jesus, we can come to the Father again and again, finding forgiveness and a fresh start. "Great is thy faithfulness" is not a hymn for people who have never failed; it is a song for people who have discovered that God has never failed them.

### Illustration

Yaw is a trader at Kejetia market. When business was slow last year, he began waking up each day with a heavy chest, already counting losses before he got out of bed. His wife suggested a simple habit: before saying anything else in the morning, they would each name one new mercy from God.

At first it felt forced. "I'm alive." "The baby slept." "We have water today." But slowly it became their favourite part of the day. Business did not suddenly boom; some months were still tight. But Yaw says the habit changed how he faced each day. "I used to start the day with my problems," he says. "Now I start it with God's mercy. The problems are still there, but they are smaller, because I see that He is bigger."

### Pray this

"Faithful God, thank You that Your mercies are new this morning. Thank You that I am not consumed, that I woke up today, and that Your compassion has not failed. Where I failed yesterday, I receive Your forgiveness through Jesus. Help me to see Your fresh mercies all through this day. Great is Your faithfulness. Amen."

### Live it today

As soon as you wake up tomorrow, before you get out of bed, name three "morning mercies": three small gifts from God that you can see right now. Do it again tonight, looking back over the day.

### Journal question

What heavy thing are you carrying from yesterday into today? What new mercy from God can you thank Him for in the middle of it?

## Day 5: The One Who Returned

> "And one of them, when he saw that he was healed, turned back, and with a loud voice glorified God, And fell down on his face at his feet, giving him thanks: and he was a Samaritan. And Jesus answering said, Were there not ten cleansed? but where are the nine?"
> (Luke 17:15 to 17, KJV)

### Reflection

Ten men with leprosy stood at a distance and cried out to Jesus for mercy. In their time, leprosy meant more than a skin disease. It meant being cut off from family, from worship and from community. Jesus told them to go and show themselves to the priests, and as they went, all ten were healed.

All ten received the miracle. Only one came back.

This man turned around and, with a loud voice, glorified God. He fell at the feet of Jesus and gave Him thanks. And Luke adds a surprising detail: he was a Samaritan, an outsider, someone the religious people of the day looked down on. The one you would least expect is the one who understood what really mattered.

Jesus asked a question that still echoes: "Where are the nine?" It is not that the nine were wicked. They were happy with the gift, but they forgot the Giver.

That is a warning for all of us. It is possible to receive God's blessings and never come back to Jesus. The one who returned shows us the heart of a worshipper: he wanted the Healer, not just the healing.

And notice what he received. In verse 19, Jesus said to him, "Arise, go thy way: thy faith hath made thee whole." All ten were cleansed, but this one went home with something more: a personal encounter with Jesus and a word of blessing from His lips. Thanksgiving brought him close to the Lord Himself.

### Illustration

After weeks of applying, Esi finally got the job she had prayed about for months. Her church cell group had fasted and prayed with her. When the offer came, she screamed, called her mother, posted on WhatsApp and went out to celebrate with friends.

A week later, her cell leader gently asked, "Esi, did you come back to thank God?" Esi was embarrassed. She had thanked her friends and her mother and even the HR manager, but she had not stopped to thank the Lord. That evening she knelt beside her bed and said, "Jesus, I'm sorry I rushed off. Thank You. This job is from You, and I want to serve You in it." She says that moment changed how she saw her work. It was no longer only her achievement; it became a place to honour Him.

### Pray this

"Lord Jesus, I don't want to be one of the nine. Thank You for every prayer You have answered and every gift You have given. Most of all, thank You for Yourself. I come back to Your feet today, not only for what You do, but for who You are. Help me to always return and give You thanks. Amen."

### Live it today

Think of one answered prayer you never came back to thank God for. Take five minutes today to "turn back": thank Him for it out loud, and if it fits, share the testimony with someone so that God is glorified.

### Journal question

Which recent blessing have you enjoyed without returning to thank the Giver? What would it look like for you to want the Healer, not only the healing?
